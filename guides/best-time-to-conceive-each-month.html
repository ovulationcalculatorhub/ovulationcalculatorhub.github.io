The Best Time to Conceive Each Month

How to time intercourse around your fertile window, how often to try, and what the evidence says about timing.

If you are trying to conceive, timing is the one factor you can control completely, and it matters more than most people expect. Conception is only possible on about six days each cycle, and the odds differ sharply between those days. Getting the timing right does not guarantee pregnancy, but getting it wrong guarantees a wasted month. This guide explains the best days, how often to try, and how to keep the process sustainable.

The six-day window

Conception is possible on the five days before ovulation and on ovulation day itself, because sperm can survive for up to five days while an egg survives for about a day. Outside that window, the chance is close to zero. The best days within the window are the two to three days before ovulation, when sperm deposited then is still alive and ready when the egg is released. Ovulation day is also fertile, but slightly less so, because the egg's short life leaves less margin. The earliest days of the window have a lower but real chance.

How often to try

Trying every day is not necessary, and for many couples it adds pressure without adding much chance. Every other day across the fertile window is enough, because sperm survives for days, so gaps are covered. Some couples prefer daily trying during the two or three most fertile days, which is also fine. What matters is covering the days before ovulation, not the total number of attempts. If trying on a schedule becomes stressful, step back to every other day across the window and keep the rest of the month normal.

Finding the window in practice

Start with the calculator: your last period date and average cycle length give an estimated ovulation date, and the window is the five days before it plus ovulation day. Then confirm with body signs. Clear, stretchy cervical mucus is the best day-to-day signal, and a positive ovulation test means ovulation is likely within 24 to 36 hours. When mucus and tests agree with the calendar, you can time with confidence. When they disagree, follow the body signs, because they describe this cycle rather than an average.

What does not help timing

Some advice about timing has no good evidence behind it. Particular positions, lying still afterwards, or timing intercourse to the hour do not meaningfully change the odds. What does matter is that sperm is present in the days before ovulation, that both partners are healthy, and that the pattern is sustainable over months. Avoid lubricants that are not sperm-friendly during the fertile window, because some ordinary lubricants can slow sperm down. Beyond that, the simplest plan is the best one: cover the window, then live normally.

When timing is not the issue

Even with perfect timing, conception in a single cycle is not certain: for a healthy couple in their 20s, the chance per cycle is roughly one in four, and it falls with age. That means several months of well-timed trying without success is normal, not a sign of a problem. If you have been timing well for a year without conceiving, or six months if you are 35 or older, see a doctor for a check. Bring your cycle records, because they show whether ovulation is happening and whether timing was actually right.

A month planned around the window

Take a 28-day cycle with the last period starting on 1 May. The calculator estimates ovulation on 15 May, with the fertile window from 10 to 15 May and the peak days 12 to 14 May. A practical plan is to try on 10, 12 and 14 May, every other day across the window, which covers the peak days without daily pressure. If mucus turns clear and stretchy on 11 May, a day earlier than expected, bring the plan forward and try on 11 and 13 May as well, because the body sign describes this cycle, not the average. After the window closes, stop timing and live normally until the next period is due. The plan is simple, it covers the best days, and it leaves the rest of the month free.

Tip: Aim at the two to three days before ovulation — sperm deposited then is still alive and waiting when the egg is released, which is why those days beat ovulation day itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best days to try to conceive?

The two to three days before ovulation have the highest conception rates, followed by ovulation day itself.

How often should we try during the fertile window?

Every other day across the window is enough. Daily trying during the peak days is also fine if it does not add stress.

Does timing guarantee pregnancy?

No. Even with perfect timing, the chance per cycle is roughly one in four for a healthy couple in their 20s, and it falls with age.

Cervical Mucus and Fertility

What fertile mucus looks like, how it changes across your cycle, and how to use it to find your fertile days.

Cervical mucus is the fluid produced by the cervix, and its texture changes through the cycle in step with your hormones. In the days before ovulation it becomes clear, slippery and stretchy, creating the conditions sperm need to survive and travel. Learning to recognise that change is one of the simplest and most reliable ways to spot your fertile window, and it needs no equipment at all. This guide explains what to look for and how to track it.

What mucus does across the cycle

After your period, you may notice little or no mucus for a few days, and the feeling is dry. As oestrogen rises, mucus appears and gradually becomes wetter, first creamy or cloudy, then clearer and more slippery. In the one or two days before ovulation it is at its most fertile: clear, stretchy, and similar in texture to raw egg white. It can stretch several centimetres between your fingers without breaking. After ovulation, progesterone makes it thicker, stickier and cloudier again, and it often becomes scant until your next period.

How to check it

The simplest check is to notice what you see on toilet paper when you wipe, before and after urinating. You can also check with clean fingers at the vaginal opening. Do this at the same time each day, ideally in the afternoon, and record what you find: dry, sticky, creamy, or clear and stretchy. Avoid checking just after intercourse or after using lubricants, because both can confuse the picture. After a few weeks of daily notes, the pattern becomes obvious, and you will recognise your own fertile mucus immediately.

Why fertile mucus matters

Fertile mucus does more than signal ovulation: it actively helps conception. It provides a channel for sperm to swim through the cervix, filters out weaker sperm, and nourishes sperm so they can survive for up to five days while waiting for the egg. Without it, sperm survive only a few hours. That is why the fertile window opens when mucus appears, not just on ovulation day, and why the clearest, stretchiest mucus is the best sign that your most fertile days have arrived.

When mucus is confusing

Some things make mucus harder to read. Antihistamines and some cold medicines can dry it up. Vaginal infections change its colour and smell, and any mucus that is yellow or green, smells unpleasant, or comes with itching should be checked by a doctor. Hormonal contraception usually suppresses the mucus pattern entirely. Breastfeeding and the weeks after stopping contraception can also make the pattern unclear at first. If mucus is hard to read for several months, combine it with ovulation tests rather than relying on it alone.

Using mucus tracking to plan

Record your daily observation in the same place as your period dates, using simple words you understand. When you see clear, stretchy mucus for the first time in a cycle, treat that as the start of your fertile window. The last day of that mucus is often the day of ovulation itself, or the day before. Over three cycles you will learn how many days of fertile mucus you usually have and when it appears, and you can use that knowledge to plan ahead. Combined with the calendar, it turns a rough estimate into a personal, evidence-based prediction.

A day-by-day example across a cycle

In a typical 28-day cycle, days 1 to 5 are the period, with blood masking any mucus. Days 6 to 9 are often dry, with little or no mucus. Days 10 to 12 bring creamy or cloudy mucus as oestrogen rises. Days 13 and 14 bring clear, stretchy, egg-white mucus, the fertile sign, and ovulation usually follows on day 14 or 15. Days 16 onwards bring thicker, stickier mucus again as progesterone rises. The exact days shift with cycle length, but the sequence stays the same, which is why learning the sequence matters more than memorising dates. After two cycles of daily notes, you will recognise your own fertile mucus immediately, and you will know your window has opened without needing a calendar.

Tip: The day your mucus first turns clear and stretchy is the day to treat as the start of your fertile window — do not wait for ovulation day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does fertile cervical mucus look like?

Clear, slippery and stretchy, like raw egg white. It can stretch several centimetres without breaking.

How many days before ovulation does fertile mucus appear?

Usually one to five days before ovulation. The last day of clear, stretchy mucus is often ovulation day itself.

Can medicines affect cervical mucus?

Yes. Antihistamines and some cold remedies can dry it up, and hormonal contraception usually suppresses the pattern.
